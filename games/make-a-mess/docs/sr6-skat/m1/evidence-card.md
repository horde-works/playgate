# SR-6 Skat — evidence card

## Primary evidence

### Certification and safety objectives

- [FAA AC 21.17-4 — Type Certification of Powered-Lift](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_21.17-4.pdf)
  supplies the performance-based baseline: weight/CG limits, controllability under
  likely propulsion failures, a controlled emergency landing after loss of required
  thrust, occupant protection during a survivable impact, rapid egress, protection
  from propellers, independent energy supply and isolation of energy storage from
  the personnel compartment. These objectives drive the one-motor-out landing
  target, survival cell, obvious canopy release and low isolated battery keel.
- [EASA Special Condition for VTOL and Means of Compliance](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/product-certification-consultations/special-condition-vtol)
  and the [Easy Access Rules for small-category VCA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/easy-access-rules-small-category-vca)
  frame the aircraft as a small VTOL-capable aircraft. Commercial operation over a
  congested area points toward the Enhanced category; M1 makes no certification
  claim and uses that category only as a severity check.

### Aerodynamics, acoustics and energy

- NASA’s [Aircraft Design Implications for Urban Air Mobility Vehicles Performing
  Public Good Missions](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240005129/downloads/1714_Silva_Final_042424.pdf)
  reports reference-vehicle disk loadings from roughly 3.5 to 20 lb/ft² and explains
  that lower disk loading generally improves hover efficiency and lowers downwash.
  SR-6 M1 sits near the middle at 10.2 lb/ft².
- NASA’s [Conceptual Design of a Tiltduct Reference Vehicle for Urban Air Mobility](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210025911)
  is the configuration reference for treating a duct, proprotor and support as one
  sized propulsive unit rather than decorative rings.
- NASA’s [Investigation of Pneumatic Inlet and Diffuser Blowing on a Ducted Fan
  Propulsor in Static Thrust Operation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040001424)
  documents the real trade: a lifting shroud can augment static thrust, while duct
  geometry can impose forward-flight drag. That is why SR-6 accepts a 100 km/h cap
  and uses ducts for low-speed integration and guarding, not as free efficiency.
- NASA’s [Fundamental Noise Characterization of a Ducted Propeller in Hover](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220006288)
  shows that a hard-wall duct changes the acoustic source balance and requires
  measurement. “Ducted” is therefore not treated as synonymous with “quiet”.
- NASA’s [Electric Vertical Takeoff and Landing Aircraft Technology for Public
  Services](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205000636/downloads/2021-08-20-eVTOL-White-Paper-Final_V48.pdf)
  gives a useful public-service eVTOL energy baseline, including pack-level lithium-ion
  specific energy around 230 Wh/kg and strong sensitivity to reserve requirements.
  The SR-6 battery line is deliberately exposed as a mass-risk assumption.

### Scale and precedent

- [Jetson ONE official specifications](https://jetson.com/jetson-one) provide the
  nearest one-seat reality anchor: 2.70 × 1.60 × 1.12 m, 115 kg including batteries,
  approximately 20 minutes, 102 km/h software-limited, redundant battery propulsion,
  one-motor-out flight, autoland and a ballistic parachute. SR-6 is intentionally
  larger and heavier because it encloses the pilot and uses six 1.34 m ducts.
- [EHang EH216-S official specifications](https://www.ehang.com/ehang216s/) provide
  the compact certified urban scale anchor: 5.73 m width, 1.93 m height, 620 kg MTOW,
  30 km range and 130 km/h maximum design speed. SR-6’s 4.68 m width and 1.90 m height
  are credible for one seat without pretending to match its redundancy or maturity.
- [VoloCity 2024 official design specification](https://www.volocopter.com/cdn/assets/vnrac6vfvrab/126QfcvwGXCr1P3wDWEvVZ/5edbda0a012c43d7cbd8116740f63145/241023_SpecSheet_VoloCity.pdf)
  provides the opposite reference: 18 rotors of 2.3 m, 11.3 m rotor-rim diameter,
  about 1,000 kg MTOM and about 20 km operational range. It confirms that low disk
  loading and urban certification tend to enlarge the footprint.
- [Airbus CityAirbus NextGen](https://www.airbus.com/en/innovation/energy-transition/hybrid-and-electric-flight/cityairbus-nextgen)
  is a reference for making distributed propulsion, lifting structure and passenger
  cell read as one aircraft architecture rather than attached gadgets.
- The [FIA Formula 1 technical regulations](https://www.fia.com/regulation/fia-formula-1-world-championship-technical-regulations)
  define a continuous survival cell and explicit rollover/side-impact protection.
  SR-6 borrows those structural principles and the reclined visual proportion, not
  any car geometry.

## Authored conclusions

- Two rows of three are kept exactly as requested. The central ducts force a hard
  2.41 m cabin bay; the cabin nose and rear taper stop before the rotor sweeps.
- The “stingray” identity comes from the plan silhouette and structural voids, not
  paint. Removing the outer torque boxes would visibly break the load path.
- Rings are justified by guarded tips, compact integration and static-lift potential,
  but their mass, drag and acoustics remain explicit liabilities.
- The rear hump carries light safety and thermal systems. Heavy batteries stay low
  and away from the pilot’s head.
- One rotor failure is the M1 design case. Anything stronger would be an unsupported
  claim until the mixer, motor ratings, thermal limit and landing trajectory are tested.
